# Theory extensions for a theory-first manuscript

**Status: 19 September 2026.** Complete bounded results with explicit proofs. These are finite, full-history adaptations of established probability and statistical design arguments, not claims to have invented stopping-time likelihood ratios, Neyman allocation, or simulation-lemma bounds. No GPU/model experiments or empirical results are needed or claimed here. The manuscript-ready version is `manuscript/sections/extensions.tex`.

The three extensions connect the evaluation design to the target: (1) define when routing may change; (2) define which prefix population a branch experiment represents; (3) quantify sensitivity when the model/harness transition law changes. The base definitions and fixed-policy inference results are in `docs/theory.md`.

## A. Exact reduction to eligible routing opportunities

Consider a complete fine-time trace with at most $N$ token/tool/execution events. Let $\mathcal F_s$ be the full history before any routing choice at event $s$. Eligibility $E_s\in\{0,1\}$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal F_s$ and follows the same prespecified rule for logging and target execution. At eligible times a router chooses a model configuration. Between eligible times both routers use the same prescribed continuation mechanism and conditional execution kernels.

For simplicity, the initial model choice is eligible. Otherwise, integrate a policy-invariant initial block into the baseline history and baseline reward. Let $\sigma_k$ be the $k$th eligible event. Require a protocol-enforced maximum of $K$ opportunities on every feasible trace, and pad missing opportunities at $N+1$ with absorbing no-ops. Let $H_k$ be the entire history before $\sigma_k$; let $A_k$ be its choice; and retain block $B_k$ from that choice through the events immediately before the next eligible choice. Retain duration, observations, and block rewards. The skeleton and its full blocks reconstruct the fine trace.

The eligibility times may depend on past routing through observed errors and other consequences. What matters is that eligibility is decided before its routing action and follows one common rule; it need not be independent of prior treatment.

**Theorem A (distributional-equivalence contract).** There are router-invariant block kernels $\mathcal K_k$ such that, for every allowed router $r$,

$$
dP^r=dP_1(H_1)\prod_{k=1}^K r_k(A_k\mid H_k)\mathcal K_k(dB_k\mid H_k,A_k).
$$

Generating and concatenating these blocks has exactly the fine-time execution law. If the target has sequential support under the logger, then

$$
W_K=\frac{dP^\pi}{dP^b}
=\prod_{k=1}^K\frac{\pi_k(A_k\mid H_k)}{b_k(A_k\mid H_k)}.
$$

If every ratio is at most $c\ge1$, then $W_K\le c^K$, $E_bW_K^2\le c^K$, and for independent traces with $|G|\le B$,

$$
\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac1n\sum_iW_{K,i}G_i\right)\le\frac{B^2c^K}{n}.
$$

**Proof.** Conditional on the full history and action at an eligible boundary, all events until the next eligible boundary are generated by a common fixed mechanism. The rule defining that next boundary also depends only on the evolving observed history. Its conditional stopped-block law is consequently independent of the router used for future eligible choices. Finite sequential conditioning produces the displayed factorization. Conversely, drawing from these same conditional block laws reproduces every fine-time conditional distribution, and hence its joint law by induction. Retaining full blocks preserves all measurable trace payoffs. The initial and block laws cancel from the target/behavior density ratio. Its expectation is one; $W_K^2\le c^KW_K$ gives the second-moment bound. Independence and $|G|\le B$ give the variance bound. ∎

The reduction counts **eligible randomizations**, not actual switches. Keeping the current model can have a policy-dependent probability and must retain its likelihood ratio. A prior observed error may define eligibility. Eventual failure, later divergence, or eventual benefit from switching cannot retrospectively select opportunities. A target with additional eligible times or a different between-opportunity controller violates the contract. Neither complete history nor elapsed time may be discarded without a separate sufficiency argument. The proof is finite factorization, not an unbounded optional-stopping argument. The likelihood-ratio foundations are established longitudinal causal inference and OPE: [Robins (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0270-0255(86)90088-6), [Jiang and Li (2016)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/jiang16.html). Related prospective designs include [Murphy (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15586395/) and [Klasnja et al. (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4732571/).

## B. Branch continuation effects, transport, and allocation

### B.1 Specify one prefix population

Fix one eligible opportunity index $k$ and obtain one padded pre-choice prefix $H=H_k$ per independent reference root episode. Denote its law by $P_B$. A target prefix router $q$ induces another probability law $P_Q$, including absorbed prefixes. Under the contract in A,

$$
w(H)=\frac{dP_Q}{dP_B}(H)
=\prod_{j<k}\frac{q_j(A_j\mid H_j)}{b_j(A_j\mid H_j)},\qquad E_Bw=1.
$$

The ratio is measurable in the full prefix and is known when routing probabilities are known. A convenience collection of visited states, or a collection conditional on failure or survival, has another law. Its density ratio requires the corresponding selection and normalization factors; the displayed prefix product cannot be reused unchanged. For example, conditional on a prefix-measurable event $F$ with positive probability under both laws, the ratio becomes $w(H)P_B(F)/P_Q(F)$ on $F$. The target normalization $P_Q(F)$ need not be known from the routing probabilities alone. Keeping one padded prefix from every root avoids this extra ratio-parameter problem. Branch selection probabilities alone do not identify a target occupancy ratio.

From a faithfully restored prefix, execute two frozen continuation policies $\nu_+$ and $\nu_-$. Let $D=G_+-G_-$ be their remaining-return difference, with

$$
\mu(H)=E(D\mid H),\qquad v(H)=\operatorname{Var}(D\mid H),\qquad
\theta=E_Q\mu=E_B(w\mu).
$$

For absorbed prefixes the remaining returns are both zero. Pairing random seeds is permitted if each continuation marginal is valid. The mean is a difference of marginal means; it does not require a unique physical cross-world coupling. The chosen valid coupling does affect $v$.

If two complete policies share prefix router $q$ and differ only in these continuations, $\theta$ is their complete-policy value contrast, because the common prefix reward cancels. For arbitrary policies with different prefixes, it is only the specified transported continuation contrast.

### B.2 Select expensive branches without changing the target

Before executing the continuation pair, select its prefix with $S\mid H\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\{e(H)\}$, independently of the potential pair conditional on $H$. Retain all sampled prefixes, including unselected ones. Condition on an external independent training sample that fixes a regression $m(H)$ and selection design $e(H)$. Assume $e>0$ where $w>0$, the required second moments are finite, and root episodes and their branch randomness are independent across roots. On $\{w=0\}$, define weighted scores, corrections, and variance integrands involving division by $e$ to be zero, including when $e=0$.

**Theorem B (selected-branch augmentation).** Define

$$
U=w(H)\left[m(H)+\frac{S}{e(H)}\{D-m(H)\}\right],
\qquad \widehat\theta=\frac1n\sum_iU_i,
$$

with correction term zero for $S=0$. Then $E\widehat\theta=\theta$, regardless of the quality of frozen $m$, and

$$
n\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta)
=\operatorname{Var}_B(w\mu)+E_B\left[w^2\left\{\frac ve+
\left(\frac1e-1\right)(\mu-m)^2\right\}\right].
$$

**Proof.** On $\{w>0\}$, conditional independence gives $E[S(D-m)/e\mid H]=\mu-m$ and $E[(S/e)^2(D-m)^2\mid H]=\{v+(\mu-m)^2\}/e$. Thus $E(U\mid H)=w\mu$ and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(U\mid H)=w^2\{v/e+(1/e-1)(\mu-m)^2\}.
$$

On $\{w=0\}$ the weighted identities hold trivially by the zero convention. Iterated expectation gives unbiasedness; total variance and independence give the stated variance. ∎

The exact variance holds conditional on the frozen external fit; fitting $m$ on the scored branch outcomes is a different calculation. Under finite nonzero variance, ordinary i.i.d. asymptotic inference uses the empirical variance of $U$. Repeated branches/prefixes from one root require a defined weighted target and cluster variance. Raw branches do not acquire the target of an ordinary root episode just because a clustered standard error is used.

**Corollary B1 (independent target-prefix sample).** Suppose there is an additional independent sample $H_1^Q,\ldots,H_N^Q\sim P_Q$. Then

$$
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{two}}=
\frac1N\sum_i m(H_i^Q)+\frac1n\sum_j\frac{w(H_j)S_j}{e(H_j)}\{D_j-m(H_j)\}
$$

is unbiased, and, with $d=\mu-m$, its variance is

$$
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_Q(m)}{N}
+\frac{\operatorname{Var}_B(wd)}{n}
+\frac1nE_B\left[w^2\left\{\frac ve+\left(\frac1e-1\right)d^2\right\}\right].
$$

**Proof.** Its expectation is $E_Qm+E_B[w(\mu-m)]=E_Q\mu$. Independence adds the two sample-mean variances. The reference-sample correction has conditional mean $wd$ and the conditional variance in the final expectation, so total variance proves the result. ∎

Target prefixes must actually be sampled or validly transported; the estimator does not identify an unsupported target-prefix law. Their generation cost belongs in any comparison. Neither estimator is claimed globally semiparametrically efficient: known prefix ratios impose structure, and other information in the full source trajectories may be exploitable.

### B.3 Allocate a fixed branch budget

Hold the reference-prefix law, number of sampled roots, and frozen $m$ fixed. Let $c(H)>0$ be expected incremental cost of running the pair, with finite positive mean. Require $\ell\le e(H)\le1$, where $0<\ell<1$, and impose $E_B(ce)\le\kappa$, with $\ell E_Bc\le\kappa\le E_Bc$. Define

$$
a(H)=w(H)^2\{v(H)+(\mu(H)-m(H))^2\},\qquad E_Ba<\infty.
$$

**Proposition B2 (allocation within this design).** Whenever a positive multiplier $\lambda$ attains the budget, the variance-minimizing selection probability is

$$
e_\lambda(H)=\min\left[1,\max\left\{\ell,\sqrt{\frac{a(H)}{\lambda c(H)}}\right\}\right].
$$

This minimizes the variances of both B and B1 with their respective sample sizes fixed. At $a=0$, use $e=\ell$. Let

$$
\kappa_{\mathrm{sat}}=E_B[c\{\mathbf1(a>0)+\ell\mathbf1(a=0)\}].
$$

A positive budget-attaining multiplier exists for $\ell E_Bc<\kappa<\kappa_{\mathrm{sat}}$. For $\kappa\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{sat}}$, use $e=1$ at $a>0$ and $e=\ell$ elsewhere; unused budget has no variance benefit. At the lower endpoint use $e=\ell$. If $a>0$ almost surely, $\kappa_{\mathrm{sat}}=E_Bc$. For an oracle regression $m=\mu$, the interior allocation is proportional to $|w|\sqrt{v/c}$.

**Proof.** The only variance term depending on $e$ is $E_B(a/e)$. Pointwise minimize $a/e+\lambda ce$ on $[\ell,1]$. Its derivative is $-a/e^2+\lambda c$ and its second derivative is nonnegative, giving the clipped expression. Its integrated Lagrangian is no larger than that of any feasible alternative. If it uses exactly budget $\kappa$, rearrangement shows its $E_B(a/e)$ is no larger than any alternative with cost at most $\kappa$. By dominated convergence, its expected cost is continuous in $\lambda$, tending to $\ell E_Bc$ at infinity and to $\kappa_{\mathrm{sat}}$ as $\lambda$ decreases to zero. The intermediate value theorem establishes existence. Once all $a>0$ prefixes have $e=1$, the objective is pointwise minimized; spending on $a=0$ is immaterial. ∎

This is a [Neyman-type allocation](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2397-2335.1934.tb04184.x) calculation, with occupancy and branch-selection weights, not a new general optimal-exploration theorem. Existing work already studies optimal allocation for sequential policy evaluation, including [Li et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02532). The formula uses **residual second moment**, not variance alone, when $m$ is imperfect. Its optimum is oracle: an independent pilot may estimate $\mu,v$ and freeze an approximate design, but estimated allocation does not automatically attain the oracle optimum. Any fixed positive-probability design retains unbiasedness. The result does not optimize root-generation cost, training cost, number of roots, or the validity of restoration.

## C. Sensitivity to model/harness changes

Let $\mathcal K_k$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal K}_k$ be two block systems on a common trace space with the same initial law, eligibility contract, and frozen routing policy $\pi$. Let total variation be $\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|$. Assume standard Borel spaces, permitting measurable conditional maximal couplings. A common trace payoff $G$ takes values in $[g_-,g_+]$, with range $L=g_+-g_-$. In particular $|G|\le B$ implies range at most $2B$.

**Theorem C (kernel sensitivity).** If at every relevant shared complete history and action,

$$
\mathrm{TV}\{\mathcal K_k(\cdot\mid h,a),\widetilde{\mathcal K}_k(\cdot\mid h,a)\}\le\varepsilon_k\in[0,1],
$$

then

$$
|v_{\mathcal K}^{\pi}-v_{\widetilde{\mathcal K}}^{\pi}|
\le L\left\{1-\prod_{k=1}^{K}(1-\varepsilon_k)\right\}
\le L\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varepsilon_k.
$$

If initial distributions have total variation at most $\varepsilon_0$, include an additional factor $1-\varepsilon_0$ in the product.

**Proof.** Start the two traces from the same draw. While complete histories agree, draw their common routing action identically. Conditional on this common history and action, maximally couple the next blocks; disagreement probability is at most $\varepsilon_k$. The probability of agreement through all blocks is therefore at least $\prod_k(1-\varepsilon_k)$. On agreement the common payoff is identical; elsewhere its absolute difference is at most $L$. Taking expectations gives the product bound; $1-\prod_k(1-\varepsilon_k)\le\sum_k\varepsilon_k$ gives the second. If initial laws differ, a maximal initial coupling supplies the extra factor. ∎

This is a finite full-history version of the classical simulation-lemma/coupling argument, not a proposed improvement on that theory; see [Kearns and Singh (2002)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1017984413808) and [Lobel and Parr (2024)](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2024/papers/Paper106.html). It controls kernels at the **same** shared histories, including model output, tools, memory, rewards, and restoration state. Model-name similarity, comparisons at unrelated prompts, or small empirical divergence do not establish its premise. An altered judge can be included in the transition by recording its output and holding payoff extraction fixed; otherwise the changed payoff itself needs an additional bound.

For a baseline $\pi_0$, write $B(\pi)$ for its policy-specific value-sensitivity bound. Subtracting the two policy bounds yields

$$
\Delta_{\widetilde{\mathcal K}}(\pi,\pi_0)
\ge\Delta_{\mathcal K}(\pi,\pi_0)-B(\pi)-B(\pi_0).
$$

A valid lower confidence bound for the original contrast can be reduced by these deterministic allowances. Under a common range and common kernel bound this subtracts twice the single-policy bound. Its interpretation is conditional on those sensitivity assumptions. Estimating usable uniform total-variation bounds from finite high-dimensional traces is unresolved here.

## Attribution and limits

The manuscript bibliography cites `robins1986new`, `jiang2016dr`, `murphy2005smart`, `klasnja2015mrt`, `neyman1934representative`, `li2023allocation`, `kearns2002near`, and `lobel2024simulation`; the primary records were checked in the parallel literature audit. The general mathematical ingredients are inherited. The explicit agent-evaluation contract and its combined use are the proposed methodological formulation, whose novelty must be assessed against related work.

These results do not remove unmeasured routing confounding, recover unsupported target prefixes, make static replay into live execution, justify arbitrary state compression, or turn selected branches into independent root episodes. They also do not establish that OPE is cheaper than direct policy execution. That comparison requires a later empirical study under the user's theory-first sequencing.
