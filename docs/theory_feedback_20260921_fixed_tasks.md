## Correct interpretation of the variance comparison

For independent task means X_g with E[X_g]=mu_g and Var(X_g)=sigma_g^2/r,

    V = Var(mean_g X_g) = sum_g sigma_g^2 / (r*n^2),
    E[s_X^2/n] = V + sum_g (mu_g - mean(mu))^2 / (n*(n-1)).

The current code evaluates these correctly. Within-task sample variances divided by r and summed with
weight n^-2 are unbiased for V when r>=2 and episodes are iid within each task. This assumption does not
automatically apply to balanced 4/4 initial assignments or shared-period execution in the archive.

Three reporting corrections are required in the next worker checkpoint/documentation; no numerical
regeneration or archival overwrite is needed:

- The reported ratio is **sqrt(E[estimated variance]/true variance)**. It is not the expectation of a
  realized standard error divided by the true standard deviation. Therefore “overstates the SE by 4–14%”
  is not established. Upward bias of a variance estimator does not by itself establish conservative coverage.
- It is not exactly independent of n. With mean stratum variance vbar and d=mu_hard-mu_easy,
  ratio^2 = 1 + r*n*d^2/(4*(n-1)*vbar). The factor n/(n-1) is close to one, but not constant.
- For the history rule across all 8 core kernels and both loggers, the actual ratio ranges are
  **1.019542–1.144032 at n=250** and **1.019484–1.143627 at n=1000**, approximately 1.02–1.14,
  rather than 1.04–1.14. These describe the preceding square-root expected-variance quantity only.

The moments are marginal single-policy quantities. A comparison of two policies evaluated on the same log
requires their score covariance; subtracting point estimates and adding their marginal variances is generally
wrong. Independent fresh-policy references instead contribute their own execution variance. No SE for one
policy should be compared directly with the adaptive advantage to claim detection power.
