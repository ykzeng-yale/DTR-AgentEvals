# Revised experimental design: evaluation, adaptation and feedback

**21 September 2026 UTC. Lead decision; prospective draft, not run authorization.** This replaces the future-design defaults in [v0.1](experiment_protocol.md); it does not retrospectively amend any archived experiment or CONFIRM outcome. New model, verifier and Monte Carlo execution remains deferred. The [literature/code review](literature_design_review_20260921.md) supplies sources and implementation boundaries. Exact resource, task and analysis manifests must pass the gates below before future collection.

## 1. Scientific questions and possible negative conclusions

The current archive demonstrates execution feasibility but does not establish history-adaptive improvement: the learned repair rule is a fixed large/small/large schedule; visible feedback and late decision opportunities are limited; several uncertainty claims remain unresolved. More training or a larger model would not distinguish these explanations by itself.

| Question | Target and comparison | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| E1: Is evaluation reliable and economical? | Values of frozen policies under a common harness; OPE versus exact truth in simulation and independent fresh policy executions in agents | Bias, error and interval calibration separately; uncertainty in the live reference; total acquisition cost for one policy and a policy catalog |
| E2: Does observed history improve allocation? | Frozen history-dependent router versus a prompt-only router with the same initial action, learner class and resources; also versus the strongest development-selected fixed schedule | Fresh root-to-terminal executions on untouched issues; prespecified success and resource comparisons, with multiplicity handled |
| E3: Are local continuation comparisons valid? | Restored-prefix continuation effects under a declared frame and execution model | Same-model restoration controls, complete state provenance and appropriate source/selection/execution inference; secondary, not required to launch E1/E2 |

Well-calibrated OPE can correctly report no useful adaptation. Good ranking need not imply accurate values. A fixed schedule winning limits the tested frozen router/class under this harness and precision; it does not rule out all useful history dependence. No significant difference is neither equivalence nor noninferiority. A failure of support is not cured by a richer outcome model.

## 2. Simulation design using known truth

Follow ADEMP: explicitly state aims, generator, target, methods and performance. Existing S1/E0 results remain historical; this design addresses additional fixed-task, opportunity and inference questions rather than relabeling those runs.

**Exact control before a larger generator.** After a common first call, draw latent repair type U with probability 1/2 and record F = U xor E, E Bernoulli(q) independently of U. At the repair decision A in {0,1}, draw a fresh terminal Bernoulli success with probability 1/2 + eta(2A−1)(2U−1); action 1 costs c. Each constant arm has success 1/2. The observable rule A=F has success 1/2+eta(1−2q), and utility gain over constant action 0 of eta(1−2q)−c/2. Use eta in {0,.2}, q in {0,.25,.5}, c=.1. These include positive, zero-information and unfavorable cost-adjusted controls. The U-observing oracle is unavailable to the learner. This is an analytic diagnostic, not an agent-performance model or a newly claimed theorem.

**Finite repair generator.** Implement explicit transition/observation tables for task stratum, latent correctness/error type, observed tool feedback, previous action, retained error-memory bit and remaining opportunities. Actions affect future state and therefore feedback and later assignment. Keep terminal correctness distinct from visible pass; a false pass may trigger the declared stopping rule. Start with binary success and bounded costs. Derive policy truth twice: full-history enumeration and Bellman recursion on a demonstrably sufficient state, including beliefs/history when observations are insufficient. Require agreement within 1e−10 for each frozen policy and each outcome component. A large simulated reference mean is not exact truth.

**Planned compact core:** n fixed tasks in {250,1000}, maximum eligible opportunities K in {2,4}, known behavior floor in {.5,.2}, no versus observable action-effect crossing, informative versus weak feedback: 32 cells. Specify actual tables and exact best-fixed versus best-observed-history advantages before accepting the generator. Changing overlap must leave targets and transition/outcome kernels unchanged; changing feedback can change policy occupancy/value and must be reported. This grid is not yet a runnable manifest. Add separately labeled severe-overlap, zero-support, hidden assignment-confounding, non-Markov coarsening, false-pass stopping and execution-drift stress cells; do not multiply every stress into the core.

**Two sampling layers, never pooled:**

1. Fixed benchmark: retain task identities across independent complete study repetitions, redraw complete assignment/execution blocks, and compute the target as a mean of task-specific expected values. An archive-matching branch module must reproduce eight episodes/task, initial 4/4 assignment, later known randomization, zero-prefix tasks, SRS prefix sampling and fresh continuations. Its pooled target is the [ratio of expected totals](theory_branch_fixed_benchmark_bound.md), not a mean of available task ratios or a selected realized-frame mean.
2. Population extension: redraw tasks from an explicit finite distribution and use its population target. Label this a distinct study. It cannot validate the archive by changing its sampling model.

**Methods:** per-decision IPW, the existing longitudinal DR implementation with known assignment probabilities, outcome regression alone, and direct on-policy evaluation. Add deliberately coarsened outcome fits and misspecified propensities as diagnostics, not production defaults. Keep zero-support and hidden-confounding cells outside identified-estimator claims. Copying outcomes and frozen-state replay are negative controls only in settings where they should fail; retain an appropriate replay positive control. Freeze policies externally for estimator calibration; refit the entire selection procedure within each repetition when studying policy selection. Keep task/family folds intact.

**Outcomes of each simulated study:** bias, MSE, interval coverage/length, inference/denominator failures, false improvement decisions, selected-policy regret, exact adaptive potential, opportunity occupancy and weight second moments. Report paired differences between methods with Monte Carlo uncertainty. Count a failed interval as noncoverage for the primary operating-characteristic summary unless the predeclared method returns a valid conservative fallback; also report failures separately. Do not hide failures by complete-case averaging.

Plan final nominal-95% coverage/type-I summaries with 2,000 independent complete repetitions: MCSE is about .0049 near .95/.05, but about .0112 near .5. If precision of .005 is required uniformly, use 10,000, subject to a separately approved compute plan. Report binomial uncertainty; neither number is a power calculation for the live study. Bias MCSE is sd(error)/sqrt(R); MSE MCSE is sd(error²)/sqrt(R). No sweep starts under this document.

## 3. Substantive agent study: two decisions inside repository repair

**Selected harness:** pinned mini-swe-agent with the pinned SWE-bench evaluator. Reuse the environment, action loop and terminal tests; implement only the routing/logging adapter and the design-specific statistics. The [harness audit](literature_harness_design_20260921.md) gives exact source hooks and hazards. BrowserGym/AgentLab on qualified MiniWoB tasks is a fallback mechanism pilot, not a replacement claim about real repository repair. Router-R1 changes the coordinator and aggregation; reserve it for an explicitly different whole-system comparison.

**Treatment:** a model choice before logical call 1 and, if still active, before logical call 9. Retain that backend for calls 1–8 or 9–24, respectively. The same message history, repository state, prompts, parser, tool permissions, stopping and context limits apply to every policy. This creates at most two eligible decisions, not 24 independent treatments. Absorbed episodes stay in the denominator. The 8/24 configuration is a development candidate, not evidence that the horizon is adequate.

**Candidate executors:** pinned Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct and 14B-Instruct; their license/revision record is in the harness audit. Resource fit, serving compatibility and competence are untested here. Prefer a common 16,384-token context and 1,536-token response cap, with a frozen decoding/quantization convention; actual converted-weight digests and server versions must be recorded. If the available host cannot support this pair, revise the development configuration explicitly rather than silently substituting models or buying API capacity.

**Feedback:** issue text, ordinary shell/tool outputs and the agent's earlier actions. A nonzero shell status is not automatically a unit-test failure. No checks means unverified, not success. Hidden terminal tests, reference patches and final grades cannot enter histories or trigger another opportunity. Preserve submission/timeout/format-limit exits. Require an explicit eligible submission: limits or format failures returning an empty submission score zero for the primary operational endpoint; do not silently extract and grade a different workspace patch. A later salvage rule would change the harness and must be declared separately.

**Prospective logging:** independent known 1/2 choices at each eligible decision conditional on recorded pre-action history; persist action, probability, draw, remaining budget and version before invocation. Offer the decision only if both backends meet the common pre-action token/request reservation rule; otherwise terminate. All target policies use this same eligibility rule. This avoids an unrecorded budget-forced action; any later single-feasible-action design needs probability 1 and corresponding target overrides explicitly specified. Hard elapsed-time limits can still interrupt execution and are recorded as exits. A deterministic supported target has a path ratio bounded by four for this two-decision logger. This is a weight bound, not a finite-sample precision guarantee. Physical transport retries keep the same assigned action; cap and log retries across all client/server layers. Never redraw the model on retry or invoke an unlogged fallback.

## 4. Comparators, training and sealed evaluation

Use all four stage schedules SS, SL, LS, LL during development; select the best fixed schedule using development data only and include SS/LL as interpretable references. Predeclare tie-breaking and the same budget eligibility rule.

The **primary history contrast** gives both candidate routers initial S. At decision 2, one can use only initial-task features; the other can additionally use realized feedback and remaining budget. Fit both with the same restricted learner class and objective, including constant actions. The proposed starting class is depth-two trees with a preregistered finite feature set and validation-selected complexity. It is a falsifiable probe of observable tailoring, not an assertion that shallow trees are optimal. Record action variability within stages: collapse to a fixed schedule is reported as such. A later richer learner is a new development comparison.

Include the published **RouteLLM local classifier as an initial-only transfer baseline**, choosing a model once for the entire trajectory. Use the pinned XLM-RoBERTa checkpoint and narrow Transformers adapter, not the API-dependent MF path. Preserve its softmax-label-0 score and score>=threshold decision. Calibrate the threshold on development data; report model-pair/domain transfer and possible pretraining contamination. Add a domain-trained initial-only router so a weak transferred baseline cannot establish superiority by itself. These are secondary comparisons, distinct from the primary matched-initial-action contrast.

Use disjoint TRAIN, development/calibration and CONFIRM issue identities; group near-duplicate/family variants. State whether claims concern new issues within named repositories or new repositories. A repository-generalization claim requires held-out repositories, not merely issue IDs. Existing MBPP/HumanEval CONFIRM observations are permanently excluded from new tuning/confirmation. Public benchmark exposure in pretraining remains a limitation.

Freeze the policy catalog, features, weights/thresholds, endpoint, task IDs, budgets, uncertainty method and contrast family before CONFIRM. Interleave policies and logging within task and execution period; record all source/execution blocks. Same seed integers do not imply a valid coupling. Use independent randomization/execution streams for logs and fresh policy references; propagate both uncertainties. No optional stopping based on an encouraging effect.

## 5. Endpoints, adequacy and resource gates

Primary outcome is **verified terminal resolution under the common execution/evaluation pipeline and budget**, with all assigned episodes in the denominator. An eligible submission with a valid resolved report scores one; other episodes score zero for this operational endpoint. Keep evaluator timeouts/missing reports separately labeled unknown algorithmic correctness, not observed hidden-test failure. Permit at most one evaluator retry on the identical patch under a common declared allowance; never regenerate a solution using grading feedback. Report the failure reasons and worst-case success bounds assigning unresolved evaluator cases either zero or one for the secondary algorithmic endpoint. Do not use complete-case exclusion. This pipeline estimand must also be used by logging and fresh-policy evaluation.

Report physical requests, small/large calls, input/output tokens, router overhead, setup/load time, inference time and total elapsed time separately. Match deterministic caps and show realized costs; equal large-call share is not equal compute. Claims of success improvement require an interval for the prespecified contrast; claims of resource saving with acceptable success require a prospectively chosen success-loss margin and a joint rule. Keep archived .01/.03 call penalties unchanged. Do not tune a new utility on CONFIRM. When the common two-action reservation rule fails, terminate as specified above.

**Infrastructure gate, later execution:** verify nonempty fail-to-pass sets, required-test mapping, a failing no-change control and a passing reference-patch control. Empty pass-to-pass sets require an explicit regression-coverage limitation. Qualify using evaluator validity, never model success. Bind each evaluation to task × policy × replicate × invocation and patch hash; existing cached reports must not substitute for new execution. Use disposable isolated workers with no project credentials.

**Development feasibility only:** after a small resource check, propose up to 20 qualified development issues × four schedules × one trajectory (80 total, at most 1,920 logical calls). Freeze a practical token/time allowance before this pilot; the worst-case output allowance is already 2.95 million returned tokens, or 5.90 million with two full physical attempts per call, before preprocessing/evaluation costs. It is not automatically cheap. No API key is needed in principle; measured local fit and throughput decide feasibility.

Report all denominators, second-decision occupancy, feedback variation, success/failure rates and infrastructure exits. Suggested redesign flags are zero successes across all schedules, >10% infrastructure failures, or <50% reaching decision 2. They are development flags, not significance tests or test-set exclusions. Do not require a favorable router effect. Failure may justify a new development configuration or a narrower paper.

**Precision gate:** the one-execution pilot cannot estimate all within-task execution variance. Obtain a separately budgeted repeated-execution development sample if needed; use its task/block covariance and uncertainty to choose the final number of tasks and replicates. Conditional on frozen trained policies and independent task execution blocks, a fixed nonidentical task mean has execution variance J^−2 sum_g Var(D_g), not iid sampling variance of task means. Shared execution-period effects require cross-task covariance terms or a justified block model. Require a reviewed design-specific interval/test, justified nuisance treatment and multiplicity control before final collection. Validate its operating characteristics in the matching simulator. Prespecify a scientifically useful gap/margin and target precision; the lead must record these numerical choices with the resource envelope before marking the plan frozen. They remain open rather than invented here.
