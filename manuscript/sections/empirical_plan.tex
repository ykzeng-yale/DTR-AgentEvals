\section{Empirical validation and remaining evaluation}
\label{sec:empirical-plan}

\paragraph{Status.}
Section~\ref{sec:coding-case-study} now reports a descriptive analysis of the existing coding-study archive.
Scoped known-truth validation and an unsuccessful repository-repair development pilot are now available,
as summarized below; the broader validation grid and prospective routing evaluation remain incomplete. This section separates those results from the remaining plan. The archived comparisons and retrospective diagnoses do not substitute
for prespecified validation of the final method, nor authorize new model or Monte Carlo runs.

\subsection{Scoped synthetic validation and finite-sample limitations}
A two-decision repair environment with exactly enumerated policy values was used to check fixed-benchmark
estimation. The baseline evaluation design used 250 balanced tasks with four independent executions per task;
replication and training-sample variations are identified below. Three frozen
policies (a feedback rule, a prompt-only rule, and a known-kernel-selected fixed schedule) were evaluated
under informative or weak feedback and uniform or feedback-dependent randomized logging. The latter logger
had minimum action probability 0.2. The selected fixed schedule is an oracle benchmark, not a learned policy.

An initial development batch used 200 repetitions per cell. Fitted task-split doubly robust estimation had
observed root mean squared error 0.742--0.934 times trajectory importance weighting across the 12 policy/cell
comparisons. This is scoped synthetic evidence, not a general efficiency guarantee. Fitted outcome regression
had positive biases of 0.00823 and 0.00446 for two policies in the informative-feedback, nonuniform-logging
cell. In a retrospective intervention on the same logs and folds, replacing second-stage regression by exact
continuation values reduced these biases to 0.00088 and 0.00127. This implicates second-stage estimation in
this implementation, without isolating pooled fallback as the cause or supplying a deployable oracle method.

A separately frozen fixed-score validation used 2,000 repetitions per cell. Nominal 95\% normal intervals
used within-task replicate variance for the fixed task list; offline-minus-independent-live intervals summed
the two estimated variances. Observed coverage ranges were 0.9425--0.9615 for direct execution,
0.9300--0.9575 for trajectory importance weighting, and 0.9355--0.9585 for their discrepancy. Thus these intervals
were not uniformly calibrated over the studied grid. For the weak-feedback, nonuniform-logging prompt policy,
importance-weighted coverage was 0.930 versus 0.953 using exact variance. Lower- and upper-tail miss frequencies
were 0.062 and 0.008. Correct mean variance estimation alone did not ensure satisfactory studentized tails.

A separate, frozen 200-repetition \emph{development} no-crossing cell had exact best fixed,
prompt-only, and observed-history utilities 0.72275. All 14 IPW/fresh fixed-policy biases
were within three Monte Carlo standard errors; IPW standard deviations were 1.25--3.04
times fresh standard deviations. Yet the selected catalog comparison favored history in
82/200 IPW and 48/200 fresh repetitions, while the exact best history catalog policy
was 0.0105 worse. These descriptive rankings neither test adaptive benefit nor estimate
a false-discovery rate. The first host-gate attempt stopped before sampling on a
self-match; the unchanged-code rerun remains DEVELOPMENT evidence with a protocol deviation.

A subsequent development sensitivity study used a new seed, 1,000 repetitions per nonuniform-logging cell,
and nested four- versus sixteen-execution task blocks. It preserved the original validation rather than
replacing it. More replication reduced variance-estimate variability and generally narrowed the gap from
exact-variance intervals, but residual undercoverage remained: the weak-feedback fixed schedule covered
0.932 for importance weighting and 0.935 for direct execution at sixteen replicates. Their exact-variance
coverages were 0.940 and 0.934, respectively. These selected diagnostics require Monte Carlo uncertainty and
are not evidence that every residual departure has the same cause. Additional replication changes the
execution budget and sampling distribution; it is not a cost-free interval correction.

An additional honest-split development study refitted observed-history regression on 250 independent
training tasks with four logged executions each, then evaluated on 250 distinct balanced tasks with four
logged and four independent on-policy executions per task. It used 1,000 repetitions per cell (4,000 total),
with frozen regression and fallback tables within each repetition. The original three-fold estimator and
its results were retained separately. Nominal 95\% Wald coverage over repeated training and evaluation
samples ranged from 0.936 to 0.955 for honest-split doubly robust value estimates and from 0.933 to 0.957
for their differences from fresh-policy estimates. Coverage Monte Carlo standard error is approximately
0.0069 at 0.95 with 1,000 repetitions. In the informative-feedback, nonuniform-logging cell, fixed-schedule
value coverage was 0.936; prompt-only and fixed-schedule discrepancy coverages were 0.934 and 0.933.
These departures are retained without declaring uniform calibration or assigning a common cause.
Doubly robust mean squared error was 0.555--0.922 times trajectory importance weighting on the same
evaluation records. The weakest paired squared-error comparison was only 1.71 Monte Carlo standard errors
below zero. Training required 1,000 additional episodes per repetition, shared across policy fits; thus
these comparisons do not establish equal-total-budget superiority. No failed or zero-variance intervals
were recorded. This study evaluates repeated-training operating characteristics, not coverage conditional
on every fitted regression, and does not validate overlapping cross-fitted inference.

A subsequent development diagnostic held five training fits fixed, selected by original repetition indices
0--4 before new evaluation draws. In the informative-feedback, nonuniform-logging cell, 2,000 evaluation
experiments per fit gave 10,000 experiments in total, retaining all three policies. Across the 15 fit/policy
combinations, doubly robust Wald coverage ranged from 0.9435 to 0.9600 (exact-variance diagnostic:
0.9435--0.9570); discrepancy coverage ranged from 0.9395 to 0.9615 (exact variance: 0.9420--0.9585).
At 0.95 the per-combination Monte Carlo standard error is about 0.0049. Importance-weighted Wald coverage
was 0.9225--0.9510, with paired Wald-minus-exact coverage differences as low as $-0.034$.
Fresh-policy exact-variance coverage also varied, from 0.9330 to 0.9560. These results support a scoped
description of performance for the five selected fits, without establishing validity for arbitrary fitted
regressions or explaining every earlier coverage departure. Original repeated-training results remain unchanged.

The completed studies provide scoped fitted-regression operating characteristics with remaining coverage
limitations; they do not establish general confidence-interval validity, calibrated joint branch inference,
or real-agent policy improvement. The underlying manifests, per-repetition estimates, Monte Carlo uncertainty
and retained failure records are available in the repository under
\texttt{results/v2\_sim/}; the development, null-control, fixed-score validation, replication-sensitivity, and honest-split coverage artifacts
remain separately identified. Final inference and broader empirical comparisons are still required.

\subsection{Known-truth evaluation of the statistical claims}
Finite sequential environments will vary prior-treatment-induced confounding, sample size, eligible-decision count, policy divergence, and outcome sparsity. Exact enumeration or dynamic programming will determine policy truth. Separate cells will omit a relevant historical variable, misspecify either nuisance family, alter the execution kernels, or introduce missing terminal outcomes. A factorial design should vary horizon and overlap separately; combined stress tests will be labeled accordingly.

Comparators will include direct on-policy estimation, iterated regression, per-decision importance weighting, normalized importance weighting, and the longitudinal doubly robust estimator. A deliberately naive switch/no-switch association and a fully specified static-replay rule will serve as failure controls. When the experiment concerns branch allocation, uniform branch selection and the oracle allocation will be compared with a rule estimated on independent development data. The oracle supplies a benchmark, not an implementable competitor with privileged test information.

The replay implementation will first be checked against both positive and adaptive-negative examples with known policy values, such as Section~\ref{adaptive-donor-replay}. Donor ordering, stopping, fallback and any thresholding of stochastic targets must be explicit. Failure in a specified counterexample does not require replay to perform worse on every empirical benchmark; a descriptive discrepancy from noisy live estimates is not a repeated-sampling bias estimate or a test of equal accuracy.

The primary statistical outputs will be bias, root mean squared error, confidence-interval coverage and length, false improvement declarations, and computation. Weight tails, zero-match rates, stage-specific effective sample sizes, empty nuisance-fit cells, and numerical failures will be retained. Monte Carlo uncertainty will accompany performance summaries. An interval that fails under misspecification will be reported as a failure under the relevant assumptions rather than hidden by selective reporting or undeclared truncation.

\subsection{Repository-repair development pilots and execution limitations}
A separate development pilot used eight SWE-bench Verified repository-repair tasks
\citep{jimenez2023swebench}. The task list was frozen before model outcomes from a feasibility-qualified
frame; it is not a representative benchmark sample. Each task received one fixed-backend episode with
Qwen2.5-Coder-7B and one with Qwen2.5-Coder-14B, using the same Q4\_K\_M conversion scheme,
mini-swe-agent, pinned task images, temperature zero, a 24-logical-call limit and a 16,384-token context window.
All sixteen episodes terminated: twelve reached the call limit, three exceeded context capacity,
and one returned an empty submission. No episode produced a nonempty submitted patch.
Under the frozen endpoint definitions, all sixteen were operational failures; none was eligible for
the separate algorithmic-success endpoint, so that rate is undefined, rather than zero.
No hidden-test evaluation was run because no episode yielded an eligible nonempty submitted patch.

Retrospective source and serialized-configuration inspection found that the driver applied the YAML
agent settings but omitted its model and environment sections, including the prescribed observation truncation and
format-error template. All sixteen episodes had this configuration deviation. Two of the three context
failures followed oversized tool observations; the third occurred with accumulated history without
a comparable oversized observation. This evidence identifies a concrete harness defect but does not
establish that it caused every failure or that correcting it will produce successful repairs.
The original cohort is retained as negative evidence about the executed system, with no model-capability,
routing-benefit or intended-configuration performance claim. The archived records do not independently
attest the exact remote script bytes executed. The archive and retrospective audit are available under
\texttt{results/v2\_agent/pilot\_20260922/} and
\texttt{docs/audits/pilot\_block1\_20260922\_lead.json}.
A separate development cohort used repaired source and serialized records consistent with the complete
YAML configuration, while retaining the eight tasks, model pins, decoding settings and limits.
It also incorporated deadline, cleanup and receipt repairs, so the comparison does not isolate YAML
restoration. All sixteen corrected-cohort episodes terminated: thirteen
reached the call limit, two exceeded context capacity and one returned an empty submission. Again,
all sixteen were operational failures, with no eligible algorithmic-success observations and no
hidden-test evaluation. The cohort used 352 physical requests; token usage is incomplete for two
failed requests. Independent saved-record checks reconcile assignments, grades, attempt counts and
all 59 published-file hashes in the sanitization manifest. The published configuration receipts contain
sanitized executable paths, so their canonical hashes differ from the recorded raw-payload hashes.
The manifest supports verification of the published projection; the worker's pre-publication validation
of the raw execution receipts is reported evidence, not independently reproduced raw-byte identity.
The corrected cohort and independent audit are archived under
\texttt{results/v2\_agent/pilot\_20260922\_yaml\_v1/} and
\texttt{docs/audits/yaml\_v1\_20260923\_lead.json}.

The unchanged operational result does not imply that the configuration repair had no effect.
Across the two cohorts, twelve of sixteen task--backend pairs had identical complete command sequences;
the remaining pairs had different sequence lengths or commands, and earlier tool observations could
differ despite temperature zero. Retrospective inspection found terminal command cycles in 28 of 32
episodes (periods one through six, with at least two repetitions). Of 446 command repetitions,
443 returned identical rendered observation text to the previous occurrence
and 437 as the first occurrence. These descriptive counts support investigating stagnant action loops;
they do not identify the effect of a recovery intervention or justify a larger budget.

No eligible patch is also distinct from no attempted edit. Some commands attempted file writes, but
the repeated write commands in the requests/small episodes failed with shell syntax errors before the
edit executed. Other episodes executed ad hoc Python reproducers, including a self-written test script;
no explicit repository test-runner invocation was observed. The frozen capture rule saved the final
workspace tree only for the two Submitted exits, so the final workspace state of the other thirty
episodes is unobserved in these records. We retain the Submitted-only operational endpoint and treat
all-exit workspace capture as a separately versioned diagnostic. The cohorts remain separate development
evidence about the executed systems, with no routing-benefit or general model-capability conclusion.
These exposed tasks cannot supply confirmatory evidence for a subsequently selected router.

\paragraph{Subsequent bounded development probes.}
Later probes tested whether an evaluator-qualified Astropy issue could supply a competent executor pair;
they were separate development configurations, not additional observations from either sixteen-episode
cohort. In the initial pair, both backends exhausted 24 calls without an eligible submission (0/2).
Repeated failed commands and package replacement complicated the intended feedback path. A versioned
offline-environment repair was followed by one 7B episode on the now-exposed issue. It submitted only a
reproducer: the strict evaluator applied the patch but passed zero of one required fail-to-pass tests,
while all 175 pass-to-pass tests passed. An eligible submission was therefore not a resolved issue.
One subsequent 14B episode made eleven consecutive identical successful reads, made no edit, and ended
when a 16,959-token request exceeded the frozen 16,384-token context. It produced no submitted patch
and was not graded. This was an agent/scaffold-generated context-limit operational failure, rather than
evidence that an exogenous capacity interruption prevented an otherwise successful repair.

These observations led us to close this local coding-model competence path. They do not isolate a model
effect from scaffold limitations or estimate a routing effect. The study-design decision to spend further
episodes on a pair with persistent action-loop failures was itself inefficient; it does not warrant a larger
call allowance or reinterpretation of the endpoint. The three probe archives are separately identified by
\texttt{req011}, \texttt{req012} and \texttt{req014} under \texttt{results/v2\_agent/}.
Their published-record audits are distinct from independent replay of the worker's private execution state.

\paragraph{Fallback mechanism screen.}
A separate browser-task development screen retained terminal full success as its endpoint. Scripted
controls completed the book-flight workflow, but an initial eight-seed 7B screen executed no browser
actions and achieved no full successes. After a versioned bracketed-element-ID interface repair, four
new seeds executed 46 browser actions across 64 logical calls and achieved zero of four full successes. Restricted action
syntax and feedback that omitted the read-only cause of repeated date-field errors limit a model-only
interpretation. Only one choice had been demonstrated outcome-consequential by the scripted controls;
multiple clicks did not establish multiple informative routing opportunities. The local browser path was
also closed. The two model screens remain separate, small development diagnostics, not pooled success-rate
evidence or substitutes for the repository-repair target. Their immutable records and interface limitations
are retained under \texttt{results/v2\_browser/} and the corresponding \texttt{req016}/\texttt{req017}
lead reviews. No competent two-policy routing comparison resulted from these screens.

A later source-only replacement-pair assessment remained blocked by the capacity and access configured
on the inspected worker host. This is a host-specific feasibility finding, not a global assertion that
adequate hardware is unavailable and not a model-performance result. Qualifying a competent executor
pair remains necessary before a new routing study.

\paragraph{Split-host single-model execution and evaluator controls.}
A later split-host configuration ran model inference on a Mac mini and executed generated commands
only in an isolated local repository sandbox. These were development execution probes on the already
exposed Astropy issue, not randomized routing comparisons. An initial Qwen3-4B trajectory ended after
two model calls and two actions because of a relay timer implementation defect. A separately recorded
trajectory after that defect was corrected completed four calls and four actions: two source inspections,
an edit, and an explicit submission. No reproduction or verification command preceded submission.
The four calls consumed 14,090 prompt tokens and 895 completion tokens; summed model-request time
was 76.86 seconds, while the model phase including relay, tool waits and cleanup was 268.83 seconds.
Both attempts remain in the acquisition record; these quantities do not include the full earlier setup
program and are neither prices nor estimates of production deployment savings.

A separate offline evaluation compared the submitted patch with the unmodified repository on the
same 176 declared tests. The no-change \emph{evaluator negative control} passed 175 regression tests
and failed the target test. The submitted patch passed 174 tests and failed both the target and one
previously passing empty-string regression. These are within-task test counts, not 176 benchmark tasks
or an agent baseline. The candidate did not resolve its one issue. A model completion claim and a high
fraction of passing regression tests do not satisfy the terminal-resolution endpoint.
The offline evaluator reused the existing image environment and omitted the stock package-installation
step. A subsequent known-good reference-patch control passed all 176 declared tests with exit zero
under the same image, test selection, parser and strict grading rule. Its first attempt stopped before
testing because a validator treated a Git-generated hunk annotation as changed source; that setup
failure was preserved, and a separately approved corrected attempt supplied the positive control.
Raw test output, original and prepared patch identities, and owned cleanup were independently checked.
The three controls support evaluator discrimination for this task and offline environment, not untouched
stock-harness equivalence or qualification of other task images. The already exposed issue is closed
to further model tuning or retries.

The trajectory also illustrates a design mismatch: it submitted at call four, before the proposed
second routing decision at call nine. It offered no realized second-stage treatment opportunity.
Moreover, source review found that the new execution harness terminated on any nonzero command
exit rather than delivering ordinary completed-command feedback. That behavior is unsuitable for
studying error-responsive repair; a nonzero shell status is not itself a unit-test outcome. It did not
cause this particular failure, since all four commands exited zero. A separately versioned correction
passed inert controller/worker/guardian tests, which establish neither real-model competence nor routing
benefit. Long tool output also followed the pinned scaffold's declared first/last-segment elision;
raw and delivered observations are retained separately. No measured contribution of that elision to
the failed patch is identified. These records are archived as REQ-028C6/C7 and REQ-029A/C. They qualify
parts of the execution path, not a competent model pair or the primary history-aware comparison.

\subsection{Prospective open-weight agent experiment}
The subsequent routing study will fix a model pool, prompts, decoding settings, tool interface, execution environment, stopping rules, and objective verifier. Development tasks will establish a feasible configuration and a nondegenerate outcome distribution. Before collection, the protocol will also set adequacy thresholds for visible-feedback informativeness, consequential routing opportunities and per-action support, with checks on development tasks. Handling of tasks with zero usable visible checks will be specified prospectively; the archived evaluation cohort will not be filtered retrospectively. After that choice, all evaluation tasks will be newly drawn or held out at the task-family level. Model weights and framework software will have separately documented licenses and immutable version identifiers.

The declared primary comparison is strict terminal repository resolution under a frozen history-aware
router versus a prompt-only router with the same initial small-model action, learner class and resources.
Both policies retain the common eligibility and stopping rules. All assigned episodes remain in the
operational endpoint denominator. Required tests must be observed passed; skipped tests, missing grades
and ineligible submissions do not count as verified resolution. Unknown algorithmic correctness remains
separately labeled. This study-specific strict score is not interchangeable with an official benchmark
score. A development-selected fixed schedule supplies an additional comparator; weak transferred baselines
or a comparison only against always-small cannot establish the value of observed history.

Routing opportunities will be defined prospectively. Known assignment probabilities will be persisted before generation, including availability and budget restrictions. Candidate policies will include always-small, always-large, an observable fixed-time switch, error-triggered escalation, a supported stochastic policy, and a competitive learned router. Each rule will specify its behavior when a trigger never occurs or an action becomes infeasible. A successful episode, a budget-limited failure, and an infrastructure loss of the outcome will remain distinct statuses.

Randomized logs will supply the offline estimates. Independent root-to-terminal executions under frozen target policies will supply validation estimates with their own sampling uncertainty. Branch experiments will separately evaluate continuation contrasts under an explicit reference-prefix or target-prefix distribution. Restoration fidelity will be checked against task-relevant state, and same-configuration branches will measure execution variability. Neither a branch dataset nor a repeated seed is automatically an additional independent task.

\subsection{Policy selection, resource use, and reporting}
Training, selection, and final evaluation will be separated by task or task family. The final sample size will be chosen for a prespecified precision or power target using development estimates of task-level contrast variance; it will not be justified by the number of model calls alone. Candidate classes, cost units, primary contrasts, and multiplicity handling will be frozen before confirmatory outcomes are viewed. Local token counts, elapsed time, billed cost, and energy are distinct measurements.

For a fixed task list, the target averages task-specific expected values; it does not require treating the
tasks as an iid sample from a new population. Independent complete repetitions of a fixed benchmark design
provide one sufficient observation unit for joint offline/fresh covariance when policies and nuisance fits
are frozen outside evaluation and each scored execution has the required marginal law. This requires
stationarity and independence across complete repetitions, not merely new seed labels. It can also be
expensive: increasing the number of tasks inside a block with arbitrary shared dependence does not
automatically improve a range-only concentration guarantee. Independent task/family execution groups
could support a more economical design, but need a stronger justified independence contract and their
own variance and interval validation. Between-task dispersion is not an estimate of within-task execution
variance for a fixed benchmark. The existing synthetic undercoverage results preclude assuming that an
unbiased variance estimate or additional repetitions alone calibrates a normal interval. Neither a passing
analysis implementation nor a generic limit theorem fixes a scientifically useful precision target or
demonstrates that the available harness can meet it.

The completed empirical package requires statistical operating characteristics against known truth, validated
offline/live comparisons, and a success--resource analysis with independently evaluated policy-improvement
contrasts. The descriptive tables in Section~\ref{sec:coding-case-study} fill part of this reporting structure;
they do not complete its inferential or comparative requirements. Each component will retain failures and
uncertainty. A single live benchmark comparison cannot establish repeated-sampling coverage, and a ranking
correlation cannot establish calibrated causal value estimates. If available computation cannot support the
planned precision, the study will report the attainable precision and narrow its claim.
