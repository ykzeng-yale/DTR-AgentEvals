\section{Empirical validation and remaining evaluation}
\label{sec:empirical-plan}

\paragraph{Status.}
Section~\ref{sec:coding-case-study} now reports a descriptive analysis of the existing coding-study archive.
A scoped known-truth validation is now available, as summarized below; the broader validation grid and a new
prospective agent evaluation remain incomplete. This section separates those results from the remaining plan. The archived comparisons and retrospective diagnoses do not substitute
for prespecified validation of the final method, nor authorize new model or Monte Carlo runs.

\subsection{Scoped synthetic validation and finite-sample limitations}
A two-decision repair environment with exactly enumerated policy values was used to check fixed-benchmark
estimation. Each study retained 250 balanced tasks with four independent executions per task. Three frozen
policies (a feedback rule, a prompt-only rule, and a known-kernel-selected fixed schedule) were evaluated
under informative or weak feedback and uniform or feedback-dependent randomized logging. The latter logger
had minimum action probability 0.2. The selected fixed schedule is an oracle benchmark, not a learned policy.

An initial development batch used 200 repetitions per cell. Fitted task-split doubly robust estimation had
observed root mean squared error 0.742--0.934 times trajectory importance weighting across the 12 policy/cell
comparisons. This is scoped synthetic evidence, not a general efficiency guarantee. Fitted outcome regression
had positive biases of 0.00823 and 0.00446 for two policies in the informative-feedback, nonuniform-logging
cell. In a retrospective intervention on the same logs and folds, replacing second-stage regression by exact
continuation values reduced these biases to 0.00088 and 0.00127. This implicates second-stage estimation in
this implementation, without isolating pooled fallback as the cause or supplying a deployable oracle method.

A separately frozen fixed-score validation used 2,000 repetitions per cell. Nominal 95\% normal intervals
used within-task replicate variance for the fixed task list; offline-minus-independent-live intervals summed
the two estimated variances. Observed coverage ranges were 0.9425--0.9615 for direct execution,
0.9300--0.9575 for trajectory importance weighting, and 0.9355--0.9585 for their discrepancy. Thus these intervals
were not uniformly calibrated over the studied grid. For the weak-feedback, nonuniform-logging prompt policy,
importance-weighted coverage was 0.930 versus 0.953 using exact variance. Lower- and upper-tail miss frequencies
were 0.062 and 0.008. Correct mean variance estimation alone did not ensure satisfactory studentized tails.

A subsequent development sensitivity study used a new seed, 1,000 repetitions per nonuniform-logging cell,
and nested four- versus sixteen-execution task blocks. It preserved the original validation rather than
replacing it. More replication reduced variance-estimate variability and generally narrowed the gap from
exact-variance intervals, but residual undercoverage remained: the weak-feedback fixed schedule covered
0.932 for importance weighting and 0.935 for direct execution at sixteen replicates. Their exact-variance
coverages were 0.940 and 0.934, respectively. These selected diagnostics require Monte Carlo uncertainty and
are not evidence that every residual departure has the same cause. Additional replication changes the
execution budget and sampling distribution; it is not a cost-free interval correction.

An additional honest-split development study refitted observed-history regression on 250 independent
training tasks with four logged executions each, then evaluated on 250 distinct balanced tasks with four
logged and four independent on-policy executions per task. It used 1,000 repetitions per cell (4,000 total),
with frozen regression and fallback tables within each repetition. The original three-fold estimator and
its results were retained separately. Nominal 95\% Wald coverage over repeated training and evaluation
samples ranged from 0.936 to 0.955 for honest-split doubly robust value estimates and from 0.933 to 0.957
for their differences from fresh-policy estimates. Coverage Monte Carlo standard error is approximately
0.0069 at 0.95 with 1,000 repetitions. In the informative-feedback, nonuniform-logging cell, fixed-schedule
value coverage was 0.936; prompt-only and fixed-schedule discrepancy coverages were 0.934 and 0.933.
These departures are retained without declaring uniform calibration or assigning a common cause.
Doubly robust mean squared error was 0.555--0.922 times trajectory importance weighting on the same
evaluation records. The weakest paired squared-error comparison was only 1.71 Monte Carlo standard errors
below zero. Training required 1,000 additional episodes per repetition, shared across policy fits; thus
these comparisons do not establish equal-total-budget superiority. No failed or zero-variance intervals
were recorded. This study evaluates repeated-training operating characteristics, not coverage conditional
on every fitted regression, and does not validate overlapping cross-fitted inference.

A subsequent development diagnostic held five training fits fixed, selected by original repetition indices
0--4 before new evaluation draws. In the informative-feedback, nonuniform-logging cell, 2,000 evaluation
experiments per fit gave 10,000 experiments in total, retaining all three policies. Across the 15 fit/policy
combinations, doubly robust Wald coverage ranged from 0.9435 to 0.9600 (exact-variance diagnostic:
0.9435--0.9570); discrepancy coverage ranged from 0.9395 to 0.9615 (exact variance: 0.9420--0.9585).
At 0.95 the per-combination Monte Carlo standard error is about 0.0049. Importance-weighted Wald coverage
was 0.9225--0.9510, with paired Wald-minus-exact coverage differences as low as $-0.034$.
Fresh-policy exact-variance coverage also varied, from 0.9330 to 0.9560. These results support a scoped
description of performance for the five selected fits, without establishing validity for arbitrary fitted
regressions or explaining every earlier coverage departure. Original repeated-training results remain unchanged.

The completed studies provide scoped fitted-regression operating characteristics with remaining coverage
limitations; they do not establish general confidence-interval validity, calibrated joint branch inference,
or real-agent policy improvement. The underlying manifests, per-repetition estimates, Monte Carlo uncertainty
and retained failure records are available in the repository under
\texttt{results/v2\_sim/}; the development, fixed-score validation, replication-sensitivity, and honest-split coverage artifacts
remain separately identified. Final inference and broader empirical comparisons are still required.

\subsection{Known-truth evaluation of the statistical claims}
Finite sequential environments will vary prior-treatment-induced confounding, sample size, eligible-decision count, policy divergence, and outcome sparsity. Exact enumeration or dynamic programming will determine policy truth. Separate cells will omit a relevant historical variable, misspecify either nuisance family, alter the execution kernels, or introduce missing terminal outcomes. A factorial design should vary horizon and overlap separately; combined stress tests will be labeled accordingly.

Comparators will include direct on-policy estimation, iterated regression, per-decision importance weighting, normalized importance weighting, and the longitudinal doubly robust estimator. A deliberately naive switch/no-switch association and a fully specified static-replay rule will serve as failure controls. When the experiment concerns branch allocation, uniform branch selection and the oracle allocation will be compared with a rule estimated on independent development data. The oracle supplies a benchmark, not an implementable competitor with privileged test information.

The replay implementation will first be checked against both positive and adaptive-negative examples with known policy values, such as Section~\ref{adaptive-donor-replay}. Donor ordering, stopping, fallback and any thresholding of stochastic targets must be explicit. Failure in a specified counterexample does not require replay to perform worse on every empirical benchmark; a descriptive discrepancy from noisy live estimates is not a repeated-sampling bias estimate or a test of equal accuracy.

The primary statistical outputs will be bias, root mean squared error, confidence-interval coverage and length, false improvement declarations, and computation. Weight tails, zero-match rates, stage-specific effective sample sizes, empty nuisance-fit cells, and numerical failures will be retained. Monte Carlo uncertainty will accompany performance summaries. An interval that fails under misspecification will be reported as a failure under the relevant assumptions rather than hidden by selective reporting or undeclared truncation.

\subsection{Prospective open-weight agent experiment}
The empirical agent study will fix a model pool, prompts, decoding settings, tool interface, execution environment, stopping rules, and objective verifier. Development tasks will establish a feasible configuration and a nondegenerate outcome distribution. Before collection, the protocol will also set adequacy thresholds for visible-feedback informativeness, consequential routing opportunities and per-action support, with checks on development tasks. Handling of tasks with zero usable visible checks will be specified prospectively; the archived evaluation cohort will not be filtered retrospectively. After that choice, all evaluation tasks will be newly drawn or held out at the task-family level. Model weights and framework software will have separately documented licenses and immutable version identifiers.

Routing opportunities will be defined prospectively. Known assignment probabilities will be persisted before generation, including availability and budget restrictions. Candidate policies will include always-small, always-large, an observable fixed-time switch, error-triggered escalation, a supported stochastic policy, and a competitive learned router. Each rule will specify its behavior when a trigger never occurs or an action becomes infeasible. A successful episode, a budget-limited failure, and an infrastructure loss of the outcome will remain distinct statuses.

Randomized logs will supply the offline estimates. Independent root-to-terminal executions under frozen target policies will supply validation estimates with their own sampling uncertainty. Branch experiments will separately evaluate continuation contrasts under an explicit reference-prefix or target-prefix distribution. Restoration fidelity will be checked against task-relevant state, and same-configuration branches will measure execution variability. Neither a branch dataset nor a repeated seed is automatically an additional independent task.

\subsection{Policy selection, resource use, and reporting}
Training, selection, and final evaluation will be separated by task or task family. The final sample size will be chosen for a prespecified precision or power target using development estimates of task-level contrast variance; it will not be justified by the number of model calls alone. Candidate classes, cost units, primary contrasts, and multiplicity handling will be frozen before confirmatory outcomes are viewed. Local token counts, elapsed time, billed cost, and energy are distinct measurements.

The completed empirical package requires statistical operating characteristics against known truth, validated
offline/live comparisons, and a success--resource analysis with independently evaluated policy-improvement
contrasts. The descriptive tables in Section~\ref{sec:coding-case-study} fill part of this reporting structure;
they do not complete its inferential or comparative requirements. Each component will retain failures and
uncertainty. A single live benchmark comparison cannot establish repeated-sampling coverage, and a ranking
correlation cannot establish calibrated causal value estimates. If available computation cannot support the
planned precision, the study will report the attainable precision and narrow its claim.
